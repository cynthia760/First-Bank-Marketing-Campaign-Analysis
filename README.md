# 📊 First Bank Marketing Campaign Analysis

*Data Analyst: Okeke Onyinye Cynthia*

## 📌 Project Overview

This project analyses the performance of a First Bank marketing campaign involving 11,162 customers. The objective was to understand customer behaviour, identify the factors associated with successful deposit subscriptions, evaluate campaign efficiency, and provide data-driven recommendations that could improve future marketing campaigns.

The campaign generated 5,289 subscribers, representing an overall subscription rate of 47.38%.

## 🎯 Project Objectives

- Measure the overall campaign subscription rate
- Identify customer groups with the highest conversion rates
- Analyse how previous campaign outcomes influence subscription
- Examine the relationship between call duration and conversion
- Evaluate the effectiveness of different contact methods
- Analyse the impact of repeated customer contact
- Investigate customer characteristics including age, occupation, education, marital status and account balance
- Identify opportunities to improve campaign targeting and efficiency

## 🧹 Data Cleaning & Preparation

Before performing the analysis, the dataset was cleaned and standardised to improve the reliability of the results.

Key preparation steps included:

- Standardising capitalisation across customer categories
- Splitting combined day and month fields for time analysis
- Grouping age, account balance, call duration and contact-count variables
- Correcting subscription-rate labels
- Checking customer and subscription totals
- Standardising inconsistent category names
- Recalculating percentages from subscription counts to validate the results

The final dataset contained 11,162 customer records.

## 🔍 Key Findings

### 1. Previous Campaign Performance

Customers who had successfully responded to a previous campaign recorded a *91.32% subscription rate*, making previous campaign success one of the strongest indicators of conversion.

### 2. Call Duration

Longer customer conversations were strongly associated with successful subscriptions.

- Long calls: *85.18%*
- Medium calls: *61.52%*
- Short calls: *28.67%*

Call duration should be treated as an indicator of customer engagement rather than simply encouraging agents to make calls longer.

### 3. Contact Method

Customers with verified contact information performed considerably better.

- Cellular: *54.33%*
- Telephone: *50.39%*
- Unknown: *22.59%*

This suggests that improving customer contact information could increase campaign effectiveness.

### 4. Number of Contact Attempts

Repeated contact showed diminishing returns.

Customers contacted only *1–2 times achieved a 50.63% subscription rate, compared with only **22.38% for customers contacted 11 or more times*.

### 5. Customer Age

Young customers achieved the highest subscription rate at *57.05%, followed by senior customers at **53.14%. Middle-aged customers recorded **42.35%*.

### 6. Occupation

Students and retired customers were particularly responsive to the campaign.

- Students: *74.72%*
- Retired customers: *66.32%*

These results suggest opportunities for more targeted customer propositions.

### 7. Account Balance

Customers with higher balances generally converted more successfully.

- High balance: *57.20%*
- Medium balance: *55.26%*
- Low balance: *42.70%*

### 8. Loan Status

Customers without existing housing loans were more likely to subscribe than customers with housing loans, suggesting that existing financial commitments may affect willingness to take additional products.

### 9. Education

Customers with tertiary education achieved the highest subscription rate at *54.11%, compared with **39.40%* among customers with primary education.

### 10. Campaign Timing

Subscription rates varied significantly across months. March and December recorded particularly high conversion rates, while May recorded a considerably lower rate.

Further testing would be required before concluding that the differences were caused by seasonality rather than customer selection or other campaign factors.

## 💡 Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the following actions could improve future campaign performance:

- Prioritise customers who successfully responded to previous campaigns
- Improve and verify customer contact information before campaigns begin
- Prioritise verified cellular contact details
- Introduce sensible limits on repeated unsuccessful contact attempts
- Re-score leads after repeated unsuccessful approaches
- Develop different marketing messages for different customer groups and life stages
- Train agents to identify customer needs and communicate product value effectively
- Consider customers' financial commitments when designing product propositions
- Test campaign timing across different months
- Measure long-term customer value, retention and profitability rather than subscription alone

This analysis demonstrates how customer and campaign data can be transformed into actionable marketing insights. Rather than contacting all customers in the same way, the results support more targeted customer selection, improved contact-data quality, reduced unnecessary repeat contact and more personalised campaign strategies.

These improvements could help the business use marketing resources more efficiently while improving customer conversion and long-term value.
